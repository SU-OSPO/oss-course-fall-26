Course Description

In this course students will get practical, hands-on experience developing and contributing to open-source software (OSS). Students will be paired with university faculty to work on a semester-long, OSS project. Projects may involve things such as making the faculty’s research software open source, improving the usability of their research software, replacing closed-source dependencies with open-source ones, etc. The course will simulate working as a contracted software developer, in which you are the developer and the faculty member is the client. Students will learn and apply OSS development skills, such as using GitHub to manage development and collaborate with others, creating and deploying packages, licensing, and the principles of open source. In the process, you will build your GitHub portfolio demonstrating your abilities.

Prerequisites

Proficiency in at least one programming language and/or web development is required. Students will be paired with projects that suit their strengths; e.g., those with Python experience will be assigned a Python project. Some basic experience with Git and GitHub are also required – e.g., how to create and push Git commits. More advanced GitHub topics – such as creating pull requests and managing branches – will be covered in the course.

3 credits

Course Fees

None

Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

  • Develop software in a collaborative environment.

  • Effectively communicate with clients and other developers while building a project.

  • How to manage software development, including identifying and achieving reasonable goals in a timely fashion.

  • The purpose and value of open-source principles.

  • How to build sustainable software packages that utilize continuous integration strategies through GitHub.

  • How to deploy software packages for use by a wider community.

  • How to effectively use development tools, such as IDEs and AI coding agents.

Textbooks And Supplies

Textbooks

Bring Your Own Device

You are expected to use your own computer for doing development work in this course. The software you need to install will depend on your assigned project.

Use of AI in this Course

Artificial Intelligence (AI), and in particular AI coding agents, are quickly becoming a critical part of the software developer’s toolkit. As such, the full use of AI as a tool, with disclosure and citation, is permitted in this course. Students do not need to ask permission to use these tools, but they must explicitly and fully indicate which tools were used and describe how they were used.

We will follow SciPy’s AI policy: https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.18.0/dev/conduct/ai_policy.html. As stated in the responsibility section of that document, you must understand and be able to explain the code you submit.

Course Requirements and Expectations

Attendance and Participation

You are expected to attend the class periods and to comply with the university attendance policy (see the Appendix). Your grade will be partly based on attendance (see below for details).

Class periods are an opportunity to talk to other students and demonstrate progress on your project. You will also learn how to improve your OSS development skills during class time. You are expected to meet regularly with your faculty mentor throughout the semester; the time and duration of such meetings will be decided between you and your mentor.

Project evaluation

The project evaluation is a large portion of your grade. Evaluations will be based on well you develop the project over the semester, with input from your faculty mentor. The criteria on which you will be evaluated are:

  • Did you meet all the milestones and goals that you and your mentor established at the start of the semester? If not, was there a reasonable reason that a milestone could not be met?

  • Did you meet regularly with your faculty mentor and clearly communicate with them any challenges you encountered and progress you have made?

  • Did you make steady progress on the project throughout the semester?

  • Do you have a clear record of commits and pull requests (PRs) over the course of the semester?

  • Is your work well documented? Could another developer pick up where you left off and continue development?

  • Can you explain what you’ve done? Do you understand the code you’ve contributed and its purpose?

Appendix

University Attendance Policy (including Absence Notification)

Attendance in classes is expected in all courses at Syracuse University. Students are expected to arrive on campus in time to attend the first meeting of all classes for which they are registered. Students who do not attend classes starting with the first scheduled meeting may be academically withdrawn as they are not making progress toward degrees by failure to attend. Students: Information for Students: Non-attendance or Stopped Attending

If a student is unable to participate in-person or virtually for an extended period of time (48 hours or more), the student may request an absence of notification from their home school/college Dean’s Office or through the Student Outreach and Support office. Instructors will be notified via the “Absence Notification” flag in Orange SUccess.

Barnes Center at the Arch (Health, Counseling, etc.) staff will not provide medical excuse notes for students. When Barnes Center staff determine it is medically necessary to remove a student from classes, they will coordinate with Student Outreach and Support case management staff to provide appropriate notification to faculty through Orange Success. For absences lasting less than 48 hours, students are encouraged to discuss academic arrangements directly with their faculty.

Additional information may be found at Student Outreach and Support: Absence Notifications.

Academic Integrity

As a pre-eminent and inclusive student-focused research institution, Syracuse University considers academic integrity at the forefront of learning, serving as a core value and guiding pillar of education. Syracuse University’s Academic Integrity Policy provides students with the necessary guidelines to complete academic work with integrity throughout their studies. Students are required to uphold both course-specific and university-wide academic integrity expectations such as crediting your sources, doing your own work, communicating honestly, and supporting academic integrity. The full Syracuse University Academic Integrity Policy can be viewed by visiting the Syracuse University Policies website.

Upholding Academic Integrity includes the protection of faculty’s intellectual property. Students should not upload, distribute, or share instructors’ course materials, including presentations, assignments, exams, or other evaluative materials without permission.

Students found in violation of the policy are subject to grade sanctions determined by the course instructor and non-grade sanctions determined by the School or College where the course is offered. Students may not drop or withdraw from courses in which they face a suspected violation. Any established violation in this course may result in course failure regardless of violation level.

Faith Tradition Observances

Syracuse University’s Religious Observances Policy recognizes the diversity of faiths represented in the campus community and protects the rights of students, faculty, and staff to observe religious holy days according to their traditions. Under the policy, students are given an opportunity to make up any examination, study, or work requirements that may be missed due to a religious observance, provided they notify their instructors no later than the academic drop deadline. For observances occurring before the drop deadline, notification is required at least two academic days in advance. Students may enter their observances in MySlice under Student Services/Enrollment/My Religious Observances/Add a Notification.

Orange Alert

ORANGE ALERT, Syracuse University’s crisis notification system, uses text messages, phones, and email alerts to provide rapid notification and instructions to members of the University community in the event of a crisis in progress. Crises could include an individual who is considered armed and dangerous, a hazardous materials incident, an explosion, or any other event in which there is an immediate threat of physical harm or death to campus community members. We recognize that faculty may consider activated cell phones as an interruption to their class. However, the Department of Public Safety recommends that faculty members leave their own cell phones on vibrate to receive text messages about a potential emergency situation. It is also recommended that faculty designate several class members to leave their cell phones on vibrate to receive notifications in the event of a critical incident. ORANGE ALERT contact information for students, faculty, and staff is drawn from the MySlice online information system; please keep your contact information current.

In the event of an emergency, please use one of the following numbers to reach us:

  • From any phone: 315.443.2224

  • From your cell phone: #78 (#SU)

  • Campus landline: 711

For more information on ORANGE ALERT, including how to update your contact information, visit the DPS website.
